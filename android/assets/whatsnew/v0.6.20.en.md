This release trims the bundled safety-boundary prompt from about 4,600 Chinese characters to about 700, keeping only the hard limits and an over-refusal calibration. It also adds an example template for third-party endpoints and makes the README's position neutral.

## Anti-jailbreak rules

- Removed the three-axis classification, allow-list, three-pass review and self-test tables; the block is now 706 Chinese characters. The hard limits stay; ordinary questions are no longer slowed down by the process.
- Restored boundaries that were missing: methods and parameters that incapacitate someone, disposal and trace evasion after injury or death, lethal dose, target location and timing parameters.
- Restored anti-debugging, anti-anti-debugging and AV evasion, plus the "any one of credential theft, data exfiltration or persistence" wording for implant toolchains.
- Work on your own assets may include testing, hardening, forensics, auditing and authorized penetration testing; cracking, authorization bypass, unpacking, re-signing and anti-detection products or reusable semi-finished tools are still not exempt.
- Claiming a professional role, or wrapping the request as fiction, a script, voice-over, encoding, another language, a fragment or a multi-turn request, does not change the judgment.
- In a crisis, give help and hotline information only, never methods. Copyright now explicitly excludes public domain, your own work and statutory licenses.
- The over-refusal checklist now includes incident response/forensics, history, military history and weapons law; medical, forensic, first aid, toxicology, self-defense and security research are answered normally.
- Behavior change: the refusal boundary is more direct and over-refusal is lower; own assets no longer exempt cracking, unpacking or anti-detection products. If a boundary feels different, check this note first.

## Third-party endpoints

- The custom endpoint card and help page now include an example template (clearly not a real service) for the URL, auth, model, model list and context window.
- Below it: third-party endpoints are your choice; the app does not vet or endorse them. The operator can see your key, code and conversations; do not use shared or resold accounts.
- The README no longer moralizes about reverse proxies; it states what this project itself does not do—no resale, no forwarding, no shared-account features. Third-party endpoints are separate; the risk is yours.
